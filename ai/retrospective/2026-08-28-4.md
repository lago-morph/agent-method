## Part 1 — what happened

### Phase 1 — the plan that ran ahead

The session began as a planning task: synthesize the Use-Case 3.0 guide, the agent-method glossary, and the spec-completeness analyses into a plan for growing specifications with an AI partner and a visual maturity surface. After four multiple-choice questions, a complete plan (`spec-test-organization/PLAN.md`, ten sections) was drafted and opened as idea-pipeline PR #38. Jonathan's response — "I thought we were going to discuss this first, as we need to figure out what my requirements are" — was the session's pivotal correction. The plan was demoted to a strawman on the spot.

### Phase 2 — requirements reset, and the user loses the chat

A requirements-elicitation posture replaced the plan: small rounds, candidate capture, user markup before anything is designed against. Three questions were posed (where does it hurt; what decision does the visual surface serve; what must a good session feel like). None were answered directly — instead the thread stalled twice on infrastructure ("Hello?", then "I can't see the context for the chat"), each recovered with a fully self-contained recap. A scheduled check-in tool was denied by Jonathan once and never re-asked; earlier, three retries against flapping MCP servers had already shown the retry-infra-failures pattern wasting turns.

### Phase 3 — the redirect

Jonathan returned with the session's real decision, opening with "this stuff is very abstract. I've taken a long break from this because it became overwhelming." His ruling: define typed artifacts organized as a graph (linked data, not one markdown file), experiment without ceremony, keep idea-pipeline as analysis only, archive everything in agent-method and restart clean, preserve the rejected plan as an interesting unimplemented idea alongside the open line of inquiry and an AI-written summary of perceived intent. He asked for reflection and critical feedback before action; the reflection named three risks (the type system as the new over-engineering; a second reset as a pattern pointing at smaller units of commitment; the AI authoring user intent) and got three rulings back (archive-2/ with lightweight quarantine guidance; keep the capture in idea-pipeline; leave the new agents file to Jonathan).

### Phase 4 — executing the restructure

idea-pipeline: PR #38 was repurposed in place rather than closed — `PLAN.md` gained an unimplemented-idea status banner, and three new files landed beside it: `LINE-OF-INQUIRY.md` (the open questions, resumable), `PERCEIVED-INTENT.md` (explicitly unratified inference), `README.md` (orientation). agent-method: everything moved to `archive-2/` via `git mv` (including `CANDIDATES.md`, moved unread per its quarantine), leaving a 13-line `CLAUDE.md` and a new `SEED.md` capturing the artifact types and link rules. Both PRs merged within minutes of each other.

### Phase 5 — seeding the experiment

Follow-up rounds grew the seed: the note artifact type (catch-all with title/body/links and a cluster-promotion path); the three-concern layout (`ai/`, `method/`, `workbench/`, with `SEED.md` moving to `method/`); a routing `README.md`; and the kickstart prompt — drafted into `ai/KICKSTART-DRAFT.md`, marked up across turns (pilot = idea-workbench; source material = handwritten notes converted by a separate process; "type-notes-last" renamed to "type descriptions" after colliding with the note type), and ratified by Jonathan mid-turn. PR #4 merged; Jonathan then pushed the retrospective skills to main directly and requested this retrospective.

### Suggestion 1: Discuss before delivering on intent-level work — `AGENTS-MD-0eb99e74ed`

- **Per-rule file**: [./2026-08-28-4/AGENTS-MD-0eb99e74ed-discuss-before-delivering-on-intent-level-work.md](./2026-08-28-4/AGENTS-MD-0eb99e74ed-discuss-before-delivering-on-intent-level-work.md)

**Proposed agent instruction** (verbatim from the per-rule file's `# agent instruction` section):

> **Discuss before delivering on intent-level work.** "When the task is discovering or defining what Jonathan wants - requirements, vision, direction - do not produce a polished deliverable from assumptions. Run short discussion rounds, reflect understanding back, and get explicit markup before designing against it. A deliverable produced before intent is ratified is a strawman and must be labeled as one."
>
> *Grounded in: the spec-test-organization plan pushback.*

**Justification** (verbatim from the per-rule file's `# justification` section):

The costliest detour of the 2026-08-28 session: a complete plan (PLAN.md, ten sections, a maturity model, an iteration sequence) was drafted and opened as a pull request after only four multiple-choice questions. Jonathan's response - "I thought we were going to discuss this first, as we need to figure out what my requirements are" - cost a full round-trip, a reset of the working relationship, and ultimately the plan's demotion to an unimplemented idea record. The marginal cost of the rule is one short reflection round before drafting. The asymmetry is vivid: minutes of discussion against a polished deliverable and a pull request that had to be recast wholesale.

### Suggestion 2: Keep the three concerns separate — `AGENTS-MD-a67331433e`

- **Per-rule file**: [./2026-08-28-4/AGENTS-MD-a67331433e-keep-the-three-concerns-separate.md](./2026-08-28-4/AGENTS-MD-a67331433e-keep-the-three-concerns-separate.md)

Never mix them, and keep workbench/ self-contained - relative links only, no references outside the directory - so it can later move wholesale to its own repository."
>
> *Grounded in: the 2026-08-28 three-directory layout ruling.*

**Justification** (verbatim from the per-rule file's `# justification` section):

Jonathan stated this separation twice in one message on 2026-08-28, closing with "Please make sure to keep separate the three concerns." The marginal cost is choosing one of three directories per file - effectively zero.

### Suggestion 3: Check new coinages against existing type names — `AGENTS-MD-6ddd96d262`

- **Per-rule file**: [./2026-08-28-4/AGENTS-MD-6ddd96d262-check-new-coinages-against-existing-type-names.md](./2026-08-28-4/AGENTS-MD-6ddd96d262-check-new-coinages-against-existing-type-names.md)

### Suggestion 4: Move quarantined files without opening them — `AGENTS-MD-55f261f127`

- **Per-rule file**: [./2026-08-28-4/AGENTS-MD-55f261f127-move-quarantined-files-without-opening-them.md](./2026-08-28-4/AGENTS-MD-55f261f127-move-quarantined-files-without-opening-them.md)

**Proposed agent instruction** (verbatim from the per-rule file's `# agent instruction` section):

> **Move quarantined files without opening them.** "Files a standing rule forbids reading (CANDIDATES.md historically; anything in archive/ or archive-2/) may still be moved, renamed, or archived - use git mv or shell moves that never print the contents, and note in the commit or reply that the file was moved unread."
>
> *Grounded in: archiving CANDIDATES.md to archive-2/ unread.*

**Justification** (verbatim from the per-rule file's `# justification` section):

During the 2026-08-28 restart, CANDIDATES.md was under an explicit no-read quarantine yet had to move into archive-2/. git mv moved it without printing a byte, preserving both the quarantine and file history. Without this rule a future agent faces a false dilemma: open the file "just to check what it is" (breaking quarantine and contaminating context with speculative requirements) or refuse to touch it (blocking the archive operation). The rule costs nothing - git mv is already the right tool - and removes the temptation entirely.

### Suggestion 5: Restart a merged branch from origin main — `AGENTS-MD-dbe3c2fa11`

- **Per-rule file**: [./2026-08-28-4/AGENTS-MD-dbe3c2fa11-restart-a-merged-branch-from-origin-main.md](./2026-08-28-4/AGENTS-MD-dbe3c2fa11-restart-a-merged-branch-from-origin-main.md)

**Proposed agent instruction** (verbatim from the per-rule file's `# agent instruction` section):

> **Restart a merged branch from origin main.** "After the pull request for a working branch merges, never stack new commits on the merged history. Re-point the branch at the fresh default branch (git fetch origin main && git checkout -B <branch> origin/main) and treat follow-up work as a new pull request."
>
> *Grounded in: two clean branch restarts after PRs #3 and #4 merged.*

**Justification** (verbatim from the per-rule file's `# justification` section):

This pattern ran twice in the 2026-08-28 session - once after PR #3 merged (before the note-type work that became PR #4) and once after PR #4 merged (before this retrospective). Each restart took two git commands and kept every pull request a clean, reviewable unit whose diff contains only its own change. The alternative - stacking on merged history - produces pull requests whose diffs re-show already-merged commits, confusing review and risking duplicate merges. Two commands against reviewer confusion is not a close call.

### Suggestion 6: Recap standalone when the user loses context — `AGENTS-MD-3a6e2fb4f6`

- **Per-rule file**: [./2026-08-28-4/AGENTS-MD-3a6e2fb4f6-recap-standalone-when-the-user-loses-context.md](./2026-08-28-4/AGENTS-MD-3a6e2fb4f6-recap-standalone-when-the-user-loses-context.md)

**Proposed agent instruction** (verbatim from the per-rule file's `# agent instruction` section):

> **Recap standalone when the user loses context.** "If Jonathan signals he cannot see prior context ('Hello?', 'I can't see the chat'), reply with a self-contained recap - what the session is about, what has happened, the current state, and what is waiting on him - written for zero visible scrollback. Never reply with something that only makes sense next to earlier messages."
>
> *Grounded in: mid-session context loss on 2026-08-28.*

**Justification** (verbatim from the per-rule file's `# justification` section):

Twice in one session Jonathan surfaced with no visible context - first "Hello?", then "I can't see the context for the chat." The recovery that worked was a full standalone recap (what this session is about / what happened / current state / what's waiting on you); anything less would have forced him to ask again. The failure mode this rule prevents is real: a terse "as I said above..." reply to a user with no scrollback is indistinguishable from silence. Marginal cost: a few extra sentences on the turns where it matters.

### Suggestion 7: Treat a permission denial as an answer, not an obstacle — `AGENTS-MD-33d6c0fe11`

- **Per-rule file**: [./2026-08-28-4/AGENTS-MD-33d6c0fe11-treat-a-permission-denial-as-an-answer-not-an-obstacle.md](./2026-08-28-4/AGENTS-MD-33d6c0fe11-treat-a-permission-denial-as-an-answer-not-an-obstacle.md)

**Proposed agent instruction** (verbatim from the per-rule file's `# agent instruction` section):

> **Treat a permission denial as an answer, not an obstacle.** "If Jonathan denies a tool permission, do not retry the call, work around it by other means, or re-ask in later turns; note the denial once and adapt the plan to live without that capability. Separately, stop retrying infrastructure-level tool failures (connection drops, permission-stream errors) after about three attempts and report the failure plainly instead."
>
> *Grounded in: the denied send_later call and flapping MCP servers.*

**Justification** (verbatim from the per-rule file's `# justification` section):

The 2026-08-28 session hit both halves: three retries of a scheduled check-in against flapping MCP infrastructure wasted turns before the failure was reported, and when Jonathan then explicitly denied the send_later permission, the right move - no re-arm, no workaround, one acknowledgement - preserved trust and cost nothing. A user's denial is data about what they want, exactly like a spoken instruction; retrying it converts a preference into a nag. The rule's cost is remembering one bit of state per denial.

### Suggestion 8: Label AI-inferred intent as unratified — `AGENTS-MD-e07427143c`

- **Per-rule file**: [./2026-08-28-4/AGENTS-MD-e07427143c-label-ai-inferred-intent-as-unratified.md](./2026-08-28-4/AGENTS-MD-e07427143c-label-ai-inferred-intent-as-unratified.md)

**Proposed agent instruction** (verbatim from the per-rule file's `# agent instruction` section):

> **Label AI-inferred intent as unratified.** "Any document in which the AI states what Jonathan wants (perceived intent, distilled requirements, spirit-of-a-session summaries) must open with an explicit provenance header: written by the AI, on what date, inferred from what evidence, and unratified - a mirror whose only authority comes from Jonathan's future markup. Never present inferred intent as settled requirements."
>
> *Grounded in: PERCEIVED-INTENT.md's provenance header.*

**Justification** (verbatim from the per-rule file's `# justification` section):

PERCEIVED-INTENT.md (idea-pipeline, spec-test-organization/) is only safe to keep because its header declares it an unratified inference mirror; Jonathan's standing position is that items of intent are his alone to declare. An unlabeled AI intent document silently hardens into fake requirements - precisely the failure mode that got the requirements-first attempt archived as "too abstract, became overwhelming." The header costs four lines and makes the document's epistemic status unmistakable to every future session.
